How to Play MelodyM8
Every piece is an instrument. Every square is a note.
What is MelodyM8?

MelodyM8 turns chess into music. Every piece is an instrument and every square is a note, so every game you play becomes a piece of music. You can play it, listen to it, train your ear with it, and even play a whole game by sound alone.

Squares are notes

The files a to g are the notes A to G. The rank is the octave: rank 1 is the lowest, rank 8 the highest. So a piece landing on e4 plays an E in octave 4, and a piece on c7 plays a high C.

The h-file is the wildcard. By default it plays a random note in that octave. You can fix it to one note in the settings.

Pieces are instruments

Pawns: piano (White) and a brighter piano (Black).

Knights: harmonica (a different harmonica for each side).

Bishops: electric guitar (White) and acoustic guitar (Black).

Rooks: tuba (White) and trombone (Black).

Queens: string ensembles.

Kings: piano.

Tap "12 Voices" at the top to hear every instrument.

How each piece sounds when it moves

Pawns and kings play one note: the square they land on.

Rooks play two notes: where they started, then where they land.

Bishops and queens play every square they glide over, like a run of notes.

Knights play their L-shaped jump as a quick cluster of four notes.

Special moments

Captures: you hear the captured piece's note cut short, then the attacker.

Castling: king and rook play together as a chord.

Promotion: the pawn's note, then the new piece's note.

Check: the pieces giving check call out.

Checkmate: the mating pieces play a chord. After a short pause, the Checkmate Symphony replays your whole game as music.

How to play

Open the Play tab. Tap a piece, then tap where it should go (or drag it).

Play against the computer or pass the device to a friend. Undo, Redo, Flip and New Game are under the move list.

Tap any move in the list to jump back and hear it again.

Checkmate Symphony

Replays any game as music, with the pieces moving on the board as it plays.

The Famous Games Gallery has classics like the Immortal Game, Fischer's Game of the Century and Carlsen's Qh6!!, ready to hear.

Export .MID saves the music as a MIDI file you can open in GarageBand, MuseScore or any music app.

Ear Training

A mystery move is played. Listen to the instrument and the notes, then guess the piece, its colour and every square it sounded on.

Paint M8

Free Play turns the empty board into an instrument: tap any square to hear its note. Pick the default tone or any piece's instrument.

Composer lets you write a tune beat by beat. Tap squares to put notes on a beat, step to the next beat, then press Play Song. Save it, loop it, or export it as a MIDI file.

Check Yourself and Wreck Yourself

Set Assist on the Play tab. Check Yourself warns you before a move that hangs a piece or lets you get mated, and lets you take it back.

Wreck Yourself is the opposite: no takebacks, and if you leave a piece hanging you hear a nasty clash.

Playing online

On the Play tab, set Mode to Online (Friend) and make a free account.

Tap Create Game and send your friend the 6-letter code. They pick Online (Friend), sign in and type the code.

Moves, clocks, resigning and draw offers all happen live. Finished games are kept under My games, ready to replay as a symphony.

Simple and Advanced

Simple shows the essentials: playing, the Symphony and the gallery, Ear Training, volume and board looks.

Advanced adds everything else: Sound-Only blindfold chess, the analysis board with arrows, the board editor, MIDI performances (like Für Elise played by chess pieces), loading PGN games, saving matches, and detailed sound settings.

Switch any time with the Simple / Advanced button at the top. Your settings are kept either way.

A tip to start

Turn the sound up, play slowly, and listen to one piece at a time. After a few games you will start to hear the board.

Where it started

MelodyM8 began as a question posted to r/musictheory and r/chess on Wednesday, 11 May 2022, titled "Musical Chess?"

"Can you potentially play chess exclusively through knowing how to hear music? And, concurrently, can you improve your ear training by playing chess and syncing a board up to music notes, chords, and instruments?"

The post imagined every piece as an instrument, every square as a note, a knight from g1 to f3 heard as G1, G2, G3, F3 (exactly how the knight still sounds today), and a whole game heard as "some kind of chaotic musical piece."
